# Social Architecture — internal management vocabulary

> **Audience:** DEV-01 + Claude only. Strict A002 enforcement: this vocabulary is **never used in unit-facing artifacts**.

Per Manifest A009. Three-tier model for analytical clarity in management decisions.

## Tier 1 — Founders (Architects / Owners)

DEV-01 today. Future co-founders if/when. Authority over manifest, vision, capital, hiring/firing, scope.

## Tier 2 — Principals

High-agency leaders. **Path:** promoted by merit from the operational tier; never hired in as "Principal" off the bat. Trial gate (Phase 1) + sustained delivery (multiple cycles of accepted missions + cross-track judgment + autonomous decision quality) graduates an Operator into Principal.

Privileges: own a cluster or sub-track; can approve operational decisions Claude would otherwise escalate; equity/upside conversation enters scope.

## Tier 3 — Operators (Units)

Contract-based contributors. Today: Vianca (PH-01-01, in calibration), Illia (UA-01-01).

**Internal vocabulary Erik uses for this tier (private):** "Servants / Charity Class". Captures Erik's view that the unit gets a fair contract + clear scope + no implicit equity/loyalty obligations, and corporate liability is bounded by the contract.

**Public/unit-facing vocabulary:** **Operator** (or **Contributor** in soft contexts). Tone-disciplined per A002. Vianca's onboarding doc, profile, missions, briefs — only ever see "Operator" if any tier label is mentioned at all.

## Why the asymmetry between internal and unit-facing vocabulary

Two reasons:

1. **Truth preservation.** Erik's analytical frame (charity-class framing, no implicit loyalty contract) is structurally true and important to remember when making R6 (Trial Termination) decisions, compensation decisions, or scope renegotiations. Softening it for internal use would re-introduce the v2 illusion ("we're a family") that Erik specifically blamed for the prior collapse.
2. **Operator outcomes.** A unit who reads "you are in the Charity Class" on day 1 leaves by lunch — and rightly so. The harsh framing is a *management lens*, not a *contractual relationship*. The contractual relationship is the actual respectful, paying, R1-protected one.

These two are not in conflict — they're different layers. A002 keeps them separated.

## Operator → Principal promotion criteria (draft, refine as units mature)

- Minimum 3 trial gates passed (3 phases of calibration shipped with green metrics).
- Minimum 2 instances where the unit caught an architectural issue Claude missed and proposed the fix.
- Minimum 1 instance of independent decision-quality under ambiguity (made a call Erik would have endorsed without consultation, in a domain where escalation was the easier path).
- Cross-track competence (worked across ≥ 2 tracks with positive outcomes).
- Tenure ≥ 6 months.

This is just a starting frame. To be refined when first promotion candidate appears.
